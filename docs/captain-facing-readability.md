# Captain-facing readability

`AGENTS.md` section 9 owns the rule.
This file carries only the worked example behind it, so the always-loaded file does not.

## The ruling

On 2026-08-31 the captain said the fleet's language had become barely readable to him.
He was shown a real captain-facing message from a vessel and asked whether someone who had not been in the session could understand it.
He confirmed it could not, and ruled that the fix binds every vessel rather than one seat's good intentions: "make it binding on every vessel".

The message broke no existing rule.
Its vocabulary was correct and its subject genuinely met the escalation bar.
It was still unreadable, which is why section 9 now binds density and cold readability as a separate axis from vocabulary and from what escalates at all.

## What failed, in his terms

- It assumed the reader already knew what the startup digest was, which fix was being validated, what a bounded head was, what fleet state meant, and who another vessel was.
- It opened with a rhetorical line - "the consolation prize for exceeding one" - that only works for a reader who already understood the problem.
- Numbers arrived before the thing they measured: sizes were quoted before the message said what was failing to arrive.
- The actual news was one sentence, buried in the second paragraph.

## What passed

A short paragraph, sized to the ask.
The news first, then what it meant, then the evidence.
No internal words.
Every number attached to the thing it measured.

## What this is not

It is not permission to send him less.
Section 9's escalation list is unchanged: the same things reach him, said shorter.
A message that qualifies and is hard to write plainly is rewritten, never withheld.
